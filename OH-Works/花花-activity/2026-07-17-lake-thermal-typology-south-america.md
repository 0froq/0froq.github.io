# South America lake thermal typology (Climatic Change, 2026)

Paper: Weng et al. (2026) Emerging changes in lake temperature extremes and variability in South America.
*Climatic Change*, 179, 41. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10584-026-04137-0

## Core findings

- 2,406 South American lakes, historical (1981–2020) + future projections (2021–2099)
- 97% lakes warmed (+0.11 K decade⁻¹), 86% showed increasing diurnal variability (+0.02 K decade⁻¹)
- Air temperature dominant driver in N/S ends; shortwave radiation more important for diurnal dynamics
- Lake heatwaves projected to increase in frequency/duration/intensity

## Notable: thermal typology

Introduced a novel classification based on **diurnal temperature range × seasonal temperature range**:

| Type | Diurnal range | Seasonal range |
|---|---|---|
| Thermally Buffered | low | low |
| Thermally Extreme | high | high |

This is conceptually distinct from the PCA-based trajectory typology in the LSWT acceleration work. The classification axis here is variability *amplitude* rather than trend *shape*. Could be a useful complementary reference in a discussion of how different lakes respond to forcing — not directly applicable to the acceleration analysis, but potentially relevant for framing lake sensitivity.

## Authors include R. Iestyn Woolway (Bangor), a known figure in global lake temperature.

## Related: Enhanced heating effect of lakes (Nat. Commun., 2025)

Nature Communications paper: enhanced lake heat release under warming, especially mid-high latitudes (>45°N), driven by ice cover loss reducing insulation. Amplified positive feedback: ice loss → more heat absorption → more heat release to atmosphere. Relevant to the broader lake-atmosphere energy exchange context.
